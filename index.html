01

Overview

First block in a sequence, not do-everything-at-once. Each 4-week block has one LEAD QUALITY that hands to the next. Block 1 leads with strength + tendon because jumping is the biggest knee aggravator (build the tendon before hammering it), heavy strength drives everything later, and it front-loads overall strength and the lean bulk. Jump work stays low and quality-focused — building the spring, not using it hard yet.

BLOCK 1 (NOW) — STRENGTH + TENDON CAPACITY → BLOCK 2 — STRENGTH-SPEED / POWER → BLOCK 3 — SPEED-STRENGTH / REACTIVE (PEAK + JUMP TEST)

All sprint + jump + Olympic-lift + heavy-squat work is consolidated onto Tue and Fri (48h+ apart), done first while fresh. Research caps quality CNS/speed work at ~2–3 sessions/week — max-effort work needs ~48h recovery, and higher plyo frequency yields worse jump gains than moderate frequency. Group the hard days, protect the easy ones. Manage hard/high-volume court sessions the same way — keep them off Tue and don't stack on Fri.

Pain Rule

0–3/10 during and after = green light. >5/10 = reduce depth/load. Rising pain across days = pull back jump volume before it becomes a setback.

Knees-Over-Toes Layer (Backward Loading)

Borrowed from ATG — builds the knee without the patellar-tendon load of jumping or deep squatting. Split into two buckets for your active tendonitis.

Adopt Now (Low-Risk)

Backward sled drag / backward treadmill / backward step-ups + knee-over-toe calf raises. Warm-up on both lower days; low-CNS knee volume on off/upper days. High reps, pain-free.

Earn It (Pain-Gated)

Deep ATG split squat and end-range knee-over-toe work. Start elevated / partial-range; deepen only as tendon tolerates (≤3/10).

05

Cleans — Rebuild Ladder

The power clean produces the highest rate of force development of any lift — higher than the CMJ or jump squat — and clean strength correlates with vertical at ~r=0.75 (bodyweight-adjusted). That's why both lower days now lead with a clean variant, waved. Counter-point: the clean is high-skill and jump squats/trap-bar jumps give similar explosive gains with less risk — so rebuild from low-skill pulls, don't grind it, keep the trap-bar jump as a fallback.

  1. Clean Pull
  2. Snatch/Clean High Pull
  3. Hang Power Clean
  4. Power Clean
  5. Full Clean
Rung-Up Rule

Advance only when the current variant is fast, clean, and pain-free.

06

Speed & Sprint Work

Sprinting is the highest-RFD, most elastic ground contact there is; it builds the horizontal force behind your first step and the run-up to a two-foot dunk, and approach speed directly raises the two-foot jump. It's also knee-friendlier than heavy depth jumps — a smart reactive stimulus during this tendon-building block.

Two Engines, One Rule

Barbell strength/hypertrophy progresses on load & reps; cleans, sprints, and plyos progress on speed & quality — gated by clean reps and silent landings. Never push the explosive work with lift-aggressiveness — that's how a tendon flares.

Failure Rule

Compounds never to failure; isolations' last set to technical failure; cleans/sprints never to fatigue — stop when speed drops.

Knee Gate

Add jump/sprint intensity next week only if pain stayed ≤3/10 and landings were silent.

08

How to Progress

Two separate progression engines run at once — never let the jump engine borrow the aggressiveness of the lift engine.

01

Engine 1 — Lifts

Volume / Load

Progress in this order

  1. Add reps in range
  2. Add load, reset to bottom of range
  3. Add a set
  4. Drop RIR (closer to failure)
  5. Deload + reset
  • Load and reps grow muscle equally — bias STRENGTH lifts (squat, incline, row) toward small load jumps; bias isolations toward reps.
  • Smallest increments possible (2.5–5 lb, +1 set). Gains come per block, not per session.

02

Engine 2 — Jumps / Plyo

Intent / Quality
  • Do NOT progress by adding reps or grinding closer to failure.
  • Progress by MORE bar/limb speed at a given load, or advancing the plyo tier — gated by clean, silent landings.
  • Tendons adapt slower than muscle: keep jump progression LAGGING the lifts, always gated by the ≤3/10 knee rule.
Two Engines, One Rule

The lifts progress on volume and load; the jumps progress on quality and intent. Never apply lift-progression speed to a tendon — that's how the knee flares.

09

Recovery & Stretching

Timing Rule

Static stretching AFTER training or later — never heavy static stretching right before jumping (it briefly lowers the tendon stiffness you want; rebounds within ~10 min). Pre-training stays dynamic; post-training is where stretching lives.

Emphasis Rule

Prioritize hips/quads/hip flexors for length. Go easy chasing calf/Achilles length — you want stiffness there for the spring; mobilize the ankle enough for dorsiflexion, no more.

Game-craft layer — Torin Dorn (@torindorn2)

Unlocks once a workout is completed today. Train the read, not the rep — every combo ends in a decision: attack, counter, or pull-up.

  • Two-ball series — same-time + alternating pounds, add a walk
  • Combo chains into a finish — 2–3 moves, end at the rim every rep
  • Pace manipulation — slow-slow-explode; the change of speed is the move
  • Cone / chair attack reads — approach, read the "defender", pick the counter
Form principles — SeeMikeDunn

  • Ball close to body — no drifting away on the lift
  • Hands underneath before the release
  • Remove unnecessary motion — optimize, don't rebuild
