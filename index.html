Court Rule

You play 3–5×/week, mostly non-live. That's fine — but live/jump-heavy runs go on Mon, Wed or Thu, never Tue or Fri. Shooting and handling can happen any day. If you played live yesterday and the knee is over 3/10, cut today's plyo to pogos only and keep everything else.

Why Three Tendon Slots

Heavy slow resistance is the best-supported treatment for patellar tendinopathy, and the protocol it comes from runs 3×/week. Tuesday's squat stays heavy-and-fast so the power quality survives, so the three HSR exposures live on Mon, Wed and Fri — 48h apart, none of them competing with your explosive day.

Session Skeleton

Every lower day runs the same five phases in the same order, so there is one thing to learn instead of four protocols to reconcile:

  • 01 Prime — backward treadmill, ISO primer, dynamic prep
  • 02 Potentiate — sprint drills, plyos, sprints
  • 03 Express — the one explosive barbell lift
  • 04 Build — the heavy or tempo strength lift plus accessories
  • 05 Restore — full ISO dose and stretching

Upper days run a shortened version: Prime → Build → Tendon Layer → Restore.

4-Week Arc

Two progression engines run at once. Barbell strength climbs on load and reps. Cleans, sprints and plyos climb on speed and quality only — never by adding reps or grinding closer to failure. The tendon work climbs by getting heavier as reps drop, on the HSR ladder.

Your ~270 squat at 185 lb is ~1.45× bodyweight. Strength-to-jump transfer is strongest below roughly 2× BW, so at your level getting stronger is still the cheapest vertical you can buy. Chase the squat toward ~370 over the coming blocks; past 2× BW, tip the emphasis toward power and reactive work.

Failure Rule

Compounds never to failure. Isolations: last set to technical failure. Cleans, sprints and plyos never to fatigue — stop the moment speed drops. The HSR tempo sets are the one place you work near your rep max, and they are deliberately slow, not explosive.

Knee Gate

Add jump or sprint intensity next week only if pain stayed ≤3/10 during and after, and landings stayed silent. Rising pain across days means pull back jump volume before it becomes a setback — the HSR work stays in regardless, it is the thing that fixes this.

Week 4 — Deload & Retest

Baselines for Block 2. Everything here is a lift or quality you actually trained this block.

Lean Bulk

~+0.25–0.5 lb/week — slow, to protect the vertical. Protein ~0.8–1 g/lb (~150–185 g/day). Re-check at each deload; if the vertical drops as weight climbs, slow the gain.

Block Sequence

Block 1 (now) — Strength + Tendon Capacity → Block 2 — Strength-Speed / Power → Block 3 — Speed-Strength / Reactive (peak + jump test). Jump work stays deliberately low here: you are building the spring, not using it hard yet.

Evidence check — what holds up and what doesn't

HoldsClean → vertical jump. Power clean 1RM correlates with vertical at about r = 0.75 once adjusted for bodyweight. Rate of force development in the power clean (~17,254 N/s) dwarfs the countermovement jump (~3,836) and jump squat (~3,517). Olympic weightlifting improves vertical jump roughly 7.7% versus control and beats traditional resistance training by about 5.1%.

HoldsModerate plyo frequency beats high. Low and moderate plyometric frequency produces greater jump and sprint gains than high frequency; two days per week generates a similar stimulus to four. This is why plyos live only on Tue and Fri.

HoldsApproach speed raises the two-foot jump. Initial horizontal centre-of-mass velocity from the run-up correlates with jump height and lets you load the stretch-shortening cycle harder. This is what the sprint work is actually for.

HoldsNordic curls. Roughly 51% reduction in hamstring strain risk (RR 0.51, 15 trials, n≈7,465). One 2021 reappraisal called the effect inconclusive on methodological grounds, but it remains among the best-evidenced items in this program. Given your sprint volume, treat it as insurance, not an accessory — most hamstring strains happen at terminal swing in high-speed running.

HoldsThe ~2× bodyweight heuristic. Strength-to-power returns climb until roughly 2× bodyweight on the bar, then flatten. At 1.45× you are nowhere near it.

WeakThe 45-minute isometric analgesia window. This traces to Rio et al. with n = 6. Replication has been mixed to negative — heavy 45s isometrics produced no meaningful acute sensory or motor benefit in Achilles tendinopathy and "cannot be recommended for immediate pain relief." Responder/non-responder heterogeneity across several tendons has undercut the one-size-fits-all version. This is why the full ISO dose moved to after the session. Prolonged submaximal isometrics also cause real central and peripheral fatigue (~19–23% MVIC reduction) — so seventeen 45-second holds before your heaviest squatting was paying a certain cost for an uncertain benefit. Two sets stay pre-session; if you are a responder you'll know, and it costs nothing.

HoldsHeavy slow resistance is the actual fix. For patellar tendinopathy, HSR outperforms both isometrics alone and eccentric training — one network meta-analysis ranked eccentric-only worst for VISA-P improvement. Kongsgaard's protocol: 3s down, 3s up, 4 sets, progressing 15RM → 12 → 10 → 8 → 6RM across 12 weeks, three times a week. That is the ladder this block puts you on.

SoftBackward treadmill / KOT work. Retro-walking trials do show greater pain reduction and quadriceps strength gains than forward walking — but the evidence comes from knee osteoarthritis populations, not tendinopathy in jumping athletes. It is low-risk, plausible, and free, so it stays as a warm-up. It is not making any promises.

Why the session is ordered this way

Everything is sequenced by nervous-system demand, highest first. Explosive work only happens on fresh legs — never at the end of a session, never after a leg-trashing day.

  1. Prime raises tissue temperature and gives the tendon a low-cost primer without accumulating fatigue.
  2. Potentiate is where quality is highest. Plyos and sprints are nervous-system work, not conditioning — they belong before anything heavy.
  3. Express is the single highest-RFD lift of the day, still on fresh legs.
  4. Build is where fatigue is allowed to accumulate, because nothing after it needs to be fast.
  5. Restore is where the full tendon dose and all static stretching live.

One stress budget: lifting, jumps and court all draw on the same recovery account. Group the hard days, protect the easy ones.

The two engines

Engine 1 — Barbell strength (volume & load)

Progress in this order: add reps in range → add load and reset to the bottom of the range → add a set → drop RIR → deload and reset. Smallest increments possible, 2.5–5 lb. Gains come per block, not per session. Bias the strength lifts toward small load jumps and the isolations toward reps.

Engine 2 — Explosive work (intent & quality)

Do not progress by adding reps or grinding closer to failure. Progress by more bar or limb speed at a given load, or by advancing a plyo tier — and only when landings stay silent and the knee stays ≤3/10. Tendons adapt slower than muscle, so jump progression should always lag the lifts.

Never apply lift-progression aggressiveness to a tendon. That is how the knee flares.

Plyometric tiers — what "advancing" means

Block 1 lives in tiers 1–5. Depth jumps are a Block 3 tool and are deliberately absent here. Advance a tier only when the current one is silent, crisp, and pain-free for a full week.

  1. Pogo hops — ankle stiffness, minimal patellar load
  2. Line / lateral hops — frontal plane, low amplitude
  3. Snapdown to stick — landing mechanics, no rebound
  4. Box jump, step down — concentric only; stepping down removes the landing impact entirely
  5. Broad jump to stick — horizontal force, controlled landing
  6. Bounds — Block 2
  7. Depth drop to stick — Block 2
  8. Depth jump — Block 3

Note that contacts are prescribed as exercises and sets, not as a contact budget. A budget invites you to fill it; a prescription doesn't.

Clean rebuild ladder

The clean is high-skill, so it gets exactly one exposure a week — Tuesday, fresh, before anything has fatigued you. Friday's power expression is the trap-bar jump instead, which is lower-skill and lets you keep pushing explosive output without a second technical lift under fatigue.

Recovery & stretching

Timing

Static stretching goes after training or later — never heavy static stretching right before jumping, since it briefly lowers the tendon stiffness you want (it rebounds within ~10 min). Pre-training stays dynamic.

Emphasis

Prioritise hips, quads and hip flexors for length. Go easy chasing calf and Achilles length — you want stiffness there for the spring. Mobilise the ankle enough for dorsiflexion and no more.

Post-court, knee first (~8 min)

  • Spanish squat ISO 2–3 × 45s — first, every court day
  • Couch stretch 2–3 min/side — mandatory for jumper's knee
  • Calf / Achilles light, 1 min/side
  • Pigeon 2 min/side · 90/90 hip switches 1–2 min

The levers, in order

  • Sleep 8+ hrs — the number one lever. Protect it hardest in Week 3.
  • Daily knee pain check, 0–10 — gates the next session
  • Honour the light days — the week is supposed to undulate
  • Fuel it — protein across the day, carbs and protein around training
  • Backward treadmill 5–10 min on off days — low-CNS circulation to the knee
What changed from v1, and why
  • Full ISO dose moved from pre-session to post-session. The evidence for pre-session analgesia is an n=6 study that hasn't replicated, and the fatigue cost is real.
  • Tendon work is now the Friday build lift, not a separate protocol. HSR is better supported than isometrics or eccentrics, and it's just tempo'd loading — so it folds into the lifting instead of competing with it.
  • One clean exposure instead of two. You can't rebuild a technical lift while performing it fatigued twice a week.
  • One heavy back squat instead of two. Friday's hack squat / leg press hits the knee extensors harder for tendon purposes without the axial load stacking next to sprints and plyos.
  • Trap-bar jump is no longer optional — it's Friday's express lift and has a real job.
  • Trap bar deadlift added. v1 tested a deadlift it never trained.
  • Plyos are prescribed as exercises and sets, not a 40–60 contact budget, and split into genuinely different qualities across the two days.
  • The hip-flexor raise and backward treadmill now live on the days you do them instead of inside the Sprints and Knee chapters.
  • Upper days differentiated by intensity zone — Monday heavy (4–6), Wed/Thu hypertrophy (8–15) — so the overlap is periodisation rather than repetition.
  • Cut: the duplicated "two engines" block, section numbering, the split between the arc and load tables, and roughly a third of the restated rules.
